A full-size three-row SUV called Q9 has joined the Audi lineup. Still, the U.S. car market is huge, the second largest behind China. Some Chinese automakers still want to find a way into such a large market, especially one in dire need of inexpensive options. Chery, for one, isn't giving up.

According to reporting from Reuters, the company is open to trying different things to get access to the U.S. market. Vice President ⁠Charlie Zhang told Reuters that Chery is confident that it can reach the U.S. market and build a car that meets the data security laws.

"Definitely from a technical point of view, we will be able to find a China-free solution in terms of hardware ​and software,” Zhang told Reuters.

One way that could possibly be accomplished is through a partnership with American automakers. However, Zhang didn’t explain to Reuters what exactly that kind of partnership would look like for Chery.

Still, this is markedly different than what other Chinese brands have said with respect to the U.S. market. Some brands, like BYD and Geely, have been open about their desire to come to the U.S., even going as far as hosting American journalists to drive their product. Yet, these companies have been clear that right now isn’t the right time to sell here; the geopolitical tensions between the U.S. and China aren’t great. Car manufacturers that already operate in the U.S. are very much against Chinese cars coming here, and there’s more legislation proposed to stop any of them from trying at all. But this isn’t stopping Chery.

Chery has an incentive to sell outside of China, though. The consensus is that Jaecoo’s products are reasonably priced, feature-packed vehicles, something that resonates with buyers on a budget. Given that the U.S. market is starved for budget-friendly choices, Chery could do well if it figured out a way to sell here.

Although right now Chery’s plan for U.S. sales is vague, Zhang did tell Reuters that Chery plans to sell in Canada next year. They're getting closer to the U.S. market, but a true entry seems to be a ways off.

#4415. PS5 "bullet ballet" game Saros gets a big update to make it both easier and harder

Bullet-dodging PlayStation shooter Saros has welcomed a sizeable Zenith update today, which has the twin aims of making the game both more accessible for those who haven't played it (or are struggling to get into it) and more replayable for those who have.

The headline additions are a quickfire boss-rush mode, a New Game Plus mode, an entire new layer of modifiers to make runs easier and harder, and - very appealingly - a slow-mo option to slow the game down.

The Slow Game Speed option can be varied depending on preference and can be activated either manually or automatically. You choose how much it slows the game down by; a video of it makes it look a lot like slow-mo in many other games, providing crucial thinking time in Saros you otherwise struggle to get.

Developer Housemarque said in a blog post it was "always wary" about toning down its games. "Although we realise our games can look intimidating at first, we didn’t want to compromise the combat system of Saros by reducing the projectiles that put the 'bullet' in 'bullet ballet'," it said. Hence the new slow game speed.

The new game modifiers allow you to tune the difficulty depending on what kind of challenge you're up for. If you want a bit of help, there are 10 new Protection modifiers you can apply. An example given was of a Corruption Melee Enhancement, which unleashes the game's deadly Corruption that builds up on you for huge melee damage.

Meanwhile, if you want to make the game harder, there are Trial modifiers. An example given was of Hostile Regeneration, which allows crowd-controlled enemies to quickly regain health unless you close to melee and finish them off with a strike.

The New Game Plus mode is called Eclipse Layers, and it activates once you've cleared the game. Each new Eclipse Layer represents a more intense version of the game with altered rules, apparently, and each has various prerequisites required in unlocking it; some require a certain amount of modifiers to be in effect, for example.

In step with this, Arjun now wears a cool sun-like crown during the Eclipse Layers - and his armour glows golden - that grows depending on the modifiers you have in play. The crown is also essential for seeing 12 new story visions Housemarque has added to the game for you to find.

Finally, Overlord Rush is the boss-rush mode that's designed to be played in 10-minute bursts, giving you a 'set' of the game's bosses to fight. A final set - Totality, it's called - has you face all eight Overlords in one continuous gauntlet.

Saros released in April on PS5 and was well reviewed, though it had a sluggish start in terms of sales, especially compared to predecessor of sorts, Returnal. Housemarque doesn't seem concerned, though, saying "this is just the start of the discussion", and that there's plenty to "tweak and add" and work will continue for "a while". This Zenith update is presumably a major part of that work.

#4414. Tested: 2027 Mercedes-Benz C-Class Provides Entry-Level Luxury

With a modest set of changes for this update, the C-class remains so good it’s a little boring.

The C-class is arguably the most important car Mercedes-Benz makes. The model line is the mass-volume driver for the automaker and a common entry point for Benz newbies all around the world. That means the company can’t mess it up. Changes and upgrades are important but must be handled delicately. For 2027, the C-class formula is unchanged; it’s an approachable baby version of what Mercedes does best.

For 2027, Mercedes tweaked the C-class’s styling, updated the powertrain, and threw a bit more tech at the car. The U.S.-bound, non-AMG, gas-powered C-class is available as a C350 with or without 4Matic all-wheel drive, but as of right now, no other models have been announced. The C350 packs a new, mild-hybrid, 2.0-liter turbocharged four-cylinder engine that uses an electric supercharger to help fill torque gaps off the line. Mercedes says it’s also adjusted the adaptive damper suspension setup on the car for reduced body roll and extra sportiness, while maintaining comfort as a central pillar. Of course, there’s also a full-electric C-class heading to the U.S., but here, we’re just focusing on the internal-combustion car.

Transmission: nine-speed automatic, rear-wheel drive

My test drive of the Euro-spec C300 primarily took place on German B-roads, bouncing from village to village. Which proved that, in short, this is a perfectly competent small sedan.

The 2.0-liter inline-four was quick enough to get me around slower traffic in passing zones and negotiate highway on-ramps without issue, but it’s far from potent. The average C-class buyer—presumably someone looking for a classy-yet-subtle small car—won’t be startled if they put their foot down, but when they need to get out of a hairy situation, its 255 hp will do just fine. I didn’t find the electric supercharger’s torque fill or the turbo’s temporary 27 hp of automatically engaged overboost to be noticeable at all, but perhaps that’s the point. This isn’t meant to be a sports car. There is adequate power when you need it, calm whenever else. Even in non-all-wheel-drive guise, it wouldn’t skirt tires on dry pavement.

The nine-speed automatic transmission remains a distinction in a segment where most rivals use the familiar ZF eight-speed auto or—in the case of the Audi A5—a seven-speed dual-clutch. This worked well under relaxed progress and certainly didn’t feel as if it was shifting too often. But in the switchable Sport mode I found it held on to lower ratios for longer than it needed to during part-throttle acceleration. Like it was trying a little too hard.

From a handling perspective, the C300 was perfectly comfortable and compliant on the road. I didn’t find any serious twisties in my short time with it, so I can’t speak much to its claimed reduction in body roll, but for general commuting purposes, I didn’t find anything to complain or brag about. Same goes for its brakes.

The interior of the C-class is largely unchanged in this update. That’s a good thing. There are screens, of course, but the gas-powered models don’t get the massive Mercedes Hyperscreen, which pretty much takes over the whole dashboard. Instead, here you get a 12.3-inch screen for the gauge cluster and an 11.9-inch infotainment display. Both were reasonably friendly to get accustomed to and navigate through.

The big update that Mercedes is touting here though is its new artificial intelligence integrations. The new C-class packs a virtual assistant that can use AI from ChatGPT, Microsoft Bing, and Google Gemini, which you can have conversations with and ask various questions. I’m not personally big on having conversations with my car, but if that’s your kind of thing, the new C-class is here for you.

With its exterior changes—lighted grille with Mercedes tri-stars there and in the lights on both ends—the new C-class is flashy enough to be cool but not too over the top to threaten its maturity.

Its optional 15-speaker, Dolby Atmos–equipped Burmester 3D Surround Sound System is also a great touch for the music lovers out there.

The 2027 C-class doesn’t stray from its formula. It’s the distilled, near-entry-level essence of the Mercedes-Benz brand. It looks like a baby S-class—which is to say, good—and has more technology than your average driver could fathom. If you’re a Mercedes loyalist, or you’re just looking to dip your toes into the world of owning (or leasing) a genuinely nice but not over-the-top sedan, you will be satisfied. But if you want something with performance focus front and center, wait for one of the AMG models to come down the pipeline. Word on the street is V-8 models are right around the corner.

This small set of changes for 2027 stands in contrast with the new G50-generation BMW 3-series and its radical Neue Klasse styling. Behind the different visual approach, the performance numbers for both cars are almost identical, with the BMW 330i having identical claimed power and torque peaks and a near-identical 0–60-mph time.

With just minor tweaks to the front end, Mercedes managed to improve the C-class’s looks significantly. I’m particularly a fan of its updated tri-star headlight design. It’s not gaudy or overly futuristic. It’s a nice, classy way of implementing the brand’s new design language and its core branding piece, while maintaining a mostly normal-looking headlight. Good work.

Even with this, an AI company would require permission from the deceased actor's family, the agency or company the actor was affiliated with, and to pay royalties.

"The Japan Actors Union will strive to not just establish these guidelines as industry-wide rules, but also strengthen its efforts to encourage the government to legislate regulations that protect 'voice rights'," wrote The Japan Actors Union (translated by Automaton). "Technological advancements will not stop, but the 'living, breathing human expression' and a 'respect for rights' must always remain at its core."

It is worth noting that these guidelines are not laws and therefore are not binding rules AI companies must follow. Even so, it marks a major push from the Japan Actors Union for this to change. One More Level’s first go at the Soulslike falls short of its premise, but it’s a confident step into the genre that leans on the studio’s prior expertise to deliver something tense and novel.

Ace Combat 8 proves the series is no museum piece; Wings of Theve soars with the best of the best, and it's a blast to play again and again.
