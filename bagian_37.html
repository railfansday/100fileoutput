#2397. Nuno’s “Self-Harming” West Ham Leave Fans Asking Serious Questions

“We just need to cut out the self harm stuff.”

That was the particularly memorable verdict from West Ham supporter John Ayris following Saturday’s 3-2 defeat to Southampton, and it probably sums up the biggest frustration surrounding Nuno Espírito Santo’s team at the moment.

John was responding beneath Martin’s player ratings, where the Hammers’ dreadful opening again dominated the discussion. West Ham were two goals down inside 11 minutes and, despite eventually producing 28 attempts, spent virtually the entire afternoon trying to repair damage they had inflicted upon themselves.

The worrying thing is that Nuno undoubtedly knows West Ham are making life more difficult with these slow starts. He repeatedly alludes to the issue, but there is a significant difference between recognising a problem and possessing the ability to fix it.

Whatever Nuno is currently trying clearly isn’t working, and I suspect he may have to do something drastic to break the nasty habbit. As I wrote immediately after the game, West Ham’s slow-start problem finally came home to roost against Southampton after weeks of getting away with it.

Los Angeles Irons went even further, writing: “I believe we have the best squad in the league. The problem has to be the manager. What would happen at other clubs if their team consistently doesn’t show up for half a game?”

I probably agree West Ham have the best squad in the second flight. Nuno certainly has more than enough talent at his disposal, yet we remain incredibly slow and I wouldn’t be surprised if we’re amongst the slowest teams in the division.

That’s going to remain a problem however strong the squad is, particularly when we’re not clinical enough to convert the huge number of chances we create.

Mukasa and Engels might have benefitted from the experience of Edson Alvarez

Trevor from Billericay made a similar point, arguing that with the squad available, “starting on the front foot with attacking intent should not be such an insolvable issue.”

George Howell was more specific, describing the decision to start without a recognised holding midfielder as “suicidal.”

For me, Southampton was probably the clearest example yet of the problem caused by not having a specialist defensive midfielder in there.

Arne Engels seemed to spend much of the game firefighting, while I actually thought Divin Mukasa was our best creative outlet. It would have been nice for Mukasa to have some security behind him to allow him to concentrate on what he does best.

I’m sure the defence would have appreciated it too because Southampton appeared capable of cutting through West Ham whenever they attacked. Martin made the same observation in his morning blog, with the lack of protection in front of the back line impossible to ignore.

Not every supporter thought West Ham were dreadful. Harvey Fox pointed out that the Hammers could conceivably have scored six or seven given the chances created, while John himself felt we should have won because of how dominant we became.

That’s almost what makes the problem more frustrating.

West Ham have enough attacking quality to overpower most teams in this division. But continually handing opponents a head start is footballing self-harm of the entirely avoidable kind.

Now he has to prove he knows how to stop it.
