#1917. Baur admits there's no hiding from Celtic's European form but is confident club will find their way again

By JOHN MCGARRY, SCOTTISH CHIEF SPORTS FEATURE WRITER

Published: 22:30 GMT, 24 October 2026 | Updated: 22:30 GMT, 24 October 2026

Mika Baur's hopes for the future in a Celtic jersey don’t prevent him from accepting the harsh reality of the present.

When a side loses four consecutive European matches but just one nine in the league, the charge which is inevitably levelled is that it’s simply incapable of coping at a higher level.

The German would dearly love to shoot the accusation down in flames and, in the fullness of time, he intends to do just that.

For now, though, the midfielder accepts that the mounting statistics are simply too damning and too convincing to contradict.

‘We lost three games,’ Baur said after the 2-1 home defeat to Celta Vigo on Thursday.

‘What should I tell you? Maybe it’s like that at the moment. But I believe in us that we can turn it around absolutely and do better against Besiktas in two weeks.

‘I don’t believe that we are not capable but, at the moment, maybe it looks like that.’

Baur tussles with Celta captain Iago Aspas in the defeat at Parkhead on Thursday night

Any team can lose a match in Europe, but when the losses are being chalked up as routinely as Celtic right now then it becomes easy to believe that the side simply don’t have the wherewithal to make an appreciable impact any more – even in UEFA’s secondary competition.

The 2-1 loss to the Spaniards followed an increasingly familiar script. Martin O’Neill’s side were out-played in the first half yet managed to strike first in the contest just after the interval.

Once Celta deservedly levelled, though, there was only ever going to be one winner.

For Celtic supporters watching their side in Europe in these days, it felt like a rerun of a dreary old movie.

Coming after he’d netted against Ferencvaros, Baur’s opening goal proved to be scant consolation.

‘Very disappointing for this to happen for the second time,’ he reflected.

‘I think it was an open first half, it was fine. Maybe they controlled it a bit more, but everything was okay.

‘Then we had a great 15 minutes in the second half and we scored.

‘We got the control, we played really, really well with the ball.

‘But they scored and the momentum switched and we were not capable to switch it again.’

The German midfielder put the Bhoys in front with his fourth goal for the club

The position Celtic now find themselves in is nothing short of desperate. With zero point and a goal difference of minus six, only Ararat-Armenia sit beneath them in the 36-team group.

With Besiktas next up in Glasgow and further challenges to come featuring the likes of Marseille and Union SG, identifying how this side somehow conjures up 10 points in five games to yet progress is inordinately difficult.

Until the point comes when rudimentary arithmetic says it cannot be done though, hope will spring eternal.

‘What else should I think? Yeah, sure,’ insisted Baur.

‘We have five games ahead, so it’s not getting any easier because we lost again. But, yes, five games with 15 points to play for, so what else?’

More immediately, the task for O’Neill’s men is to bounce back at lunchtime.

As was the case following their loss to Benfica, another opponent from the capital lies in wait with Hibernian praying they aren’t made to suffer the way Hearts were last weekend.

The Celtic manager can only hope that his players repeat the trick of parking another tough European night to pick up another three league points.

Asked what the psychological secret was as a switch was flicked in the short turnaround after Lisbon, Baur said: ‘There is no time. Maybe that’s the good point — here’s no time. The next game is on Sunday. We can be disappointed on Thursday night, the next day we do regeneration.

‘But then, by Saturday, the focus is all on Hibs.

‘So, maybe that’s the good thing, to play every three days.’

Javi Rodriguez's double condemned Celtic to a third straight defeat in the Europa League

Under Marink Reedijk, Hibs have started poorly with two losses extending the form slump which cost David Gray his job.

But Baur sees an inherent danger in assuming anything about a trip to face the division’s bottom club.

Asked if Celtic had to treat the fixture as if they were facing a top European side, the German replied: ‘Not treat them like a European game, but if you’re not doing it, it’s not possible to win.

‘It’s like that. It’s easy. If you’re not treating them like a real good opponent, it’s not possible to win that game.

‘European football is much more difficult, for sure.

‘But we’ve had really, really tough games in the league as well.

‘It’s not getting easier to go away and play St Mirren, St Johnstone or whoever. We should move on in the league, be confident, like we did last Sunday.

‘That was not an easy game. It maybe it looked like that, but it was well deserved.

‘But it’s very disappointing that we are not capable at the moment to perform like that in the Europa League.’

While the overall standard of Celtic’s recruitment is a contentious issue, there are no question marks over the initial £4.5m spent to bring Baur in from Paderborn in the summer.

Already with four goals to his name, half of which have come in Europe, the 22-year-old believes there’s more to come.

‘I try to do my best all the time,’ he said. ‘I try to help the team by scoring and hopefully deliver a few assists as well in the next games. But firstly, it’s Callum (McGregor’s) and my job in the midfield to control it.

‘We did a few times really well, a few times not. So, it’s on us to do that. But personally, I feel more confident for sure in the last weeks.’

United could now look to extend his contract to protect his transfer value before deciding whether it is time to move on from their influential skipper.

The Red Devils hierarchy are assessing the possibility of selling Fernandes next summer, according to emerging reports, with sources close to the situation indicating that his departure is now a genuine option.

The 31-year-old is approaching the end of his current contract, although United hold an option to extend his deal by a further year. The club are considering activating that clause to preserve his value before potentially sanctioning a sale.

Fernandes' situation has changed considerably in a short space of time. Despite recently being named Premier League Player of the Year and breaking the competition's record for assists in a single season, he is now facing increased scrutiny following his criticism of Carrick's tactics.

The midfielder was subsequently left as an unused substitute during their 3-0 defeat to Bournemouth, having started every previous Premier League game this season.

Fernandes has registered three goals and one assist in seven league appearances, but his performances and body language have attracted criticism in recent weeks.

Interest from Saudi Arabia could also resurface in January or in the summer, potentially giving United a lucrative opportunity to cash in. A departure is far from guaranteed, but the club appear increasingly willing to consider their options.

Fernandes has been one of United's most important players for years, but there is a growing argument that a separation could now benefit both parties.

His quality is not in question. The problem is that United's squad remains so imbalanced that successive managers have struggled to play him in his best position, as a no.10.

Carrick has used Fernandes on the right wing and up front, while Ruben Amorim deployed him in a deeper midfield role. Those adjustments have often appeared designed to accommodate other players rather than maximise the captain's creative influence.

Selling Fernandes would be a significant decision, particularly given his importance to the team, but extending his contract before considering a transfer could make financial sense.

If United can secure a substantial fee and reinvest it in players better suited to a coherent long-term system, moving on may be the right decision for everyone.

Fernandes deserves a team built around his strengths, and United need a squad that works collectively rather than one that relies so heavily on a single creative outlet.

#1844. Ballon d'Or winners by country: Where do Argentina, France, England rank?

By Oliver Thomas | 27 Oct 2026 10:15

The Ballon d'Or is football's most prestigious individual honour that has been awarded to the best player on the planet since its inception in 1956.

A total of twenty different nations have had players get their hands on the golden trophy over the years, but a select group of powerhouse nations have traditionally dominated the podium, with only five countries achieving more than five wins.

A different nationality has won each of the last four Ballon d’Or trophies, with Argentina’s Lionel Messi, Spain’s Rodri and France’s Ousmane Dembele claiming the award before England’s Harry Kane was crowned the 2026 winner.

Here, Sports Mole takes a closer look at every winner of the Ballon d’Or by country.

Argentina and France command the ultimate positions at the very pinnacle of football’s individual landscape, sitting tied at the top of the historical standings with eight Ballon d'Or victories each.

For Argentina, this modern supremacy is down to the unmatched genius of Lionel Messi – arguably the greatest player of all time – who single-handedly claimed all eight of his nation's trophies, with no other player ever winning the award on more occasions to date.

Remarkably, Argentina's footprint could be even deeper; Alfredo Di Stefano and Omar Sivori were both Argentine-born but secured their mid-century accolades under Spanish and Italian citizenships respectively.

In contrast to Argentina's single-player dominance, France's rise to the summit represents a much richer tapestry across multiple generations, with a record six different French players having hoisted the golden trophy.

From the foundational eras of Raymond Kopa and Michel Platini to the triumphs of Jean-Pierre Papin, Zinedine Zidane, Karim Benzema and most recently Ousmane Dembele, Les Bleus boast an unmatched evolutionary depth of elite talent.

Chasing the leading duo at the summit is an elite European trio consisting of Germany, the Netherlands and Portugal, all tied closely behind with seven victories each.

Like France, Germany boast historic depth having produced five unique winners, while the Netherlands and Portugal have produced three each. However, Portugal's standing relies heavily on Cristiano Ronaldo – another titan in the greatest-of-all-time debate – who claimed five of his country's seven Ballon d’Or accolades, with no European player winning the award more times than the legendary forward.

The historical heritage of the accolade remains heavily anchored in Europe due to decades of strict eligibility rules, making the joint Argentine-French era even more remarkable.

Non-European players were entirely excluded from the voting process until 1995, when George Weah’s historic victory for Liberia finally broke the barrier. Since that pivotal shift, the global map has steadily expanded, opening the door for sporadic milestones such as Luka Modric making Croatia a maiden nation in 2018.

Yet even as the 2025 and 2026 landscapes usher in a brand-new generation of global superstars like Harry Kane, the historic dominance of the traditional heavyweights continues to serve as the benchmark for international footballing excellence.

By Axel Clody | 27 Oct 2026 10:34

The Ballon d’Or does not always reflect what happened over the course of the season and often strays from the justice that the pitch actually showed.

In 2026, with the weight of the World Cup, a competition lasting only a month and eight matches, that is even more the case. Here are the three biggest injustices of a ceremony marked by Harry Kane’s triumph.

THE 2026 RANKING FOR THE MEN'S BALLON D'OR IS OUT! #ballondor https://t.co/LYfoOUJf5j

For the first time in 22 years, Arsenal won the Premier League. They also reached the Champions League final for only the second time in their more than century-long history. And yet, not one member of the side managed by Mikel Arteta finished among the 10 best players in the world.

Of course, the poor last season of Martin Odegaard and Bukayo Saka, attacking players who tend to be more highly valued by individual awards, partly explains the absence. However, the level shown by Gabriel Magalhaes, 21st in the Ballon d’Or list, and Declan Rice, 15th, deserved the presence of at least one of them in the top 10.

Rodri and Lionel Messi, for example, only made the top 10 because of a single month: the period in which the World Cup was played.

The World Cup obviously affected the list and made some inclusions unjustifiable.

Julian Quinones, 28th, is the biggest example: he shone with four goals and one assist for Mexico in the tournament, taking the hosts to the quarter-finals. However, he plays for Al-Qadsiah in Saudi Arabia, where he did not even win the league and, despite many goals scored, the football is considered below that of Europe’s top five leagues.

Sadio Mane, of Al-Nassr, 29th, is in for the same reason. He shone less than Quinones, but he won the Africa Cup of Nations, not counting the court ruling that handed the trophy to Morocco off the pitch, and the Saudi league. At the World Cup, he had an inconsistent tournament with Senegal.

Ferran Torres, 16th, is the biggest absurdity. The Spanish forward’s season, split between spells as a substitute and as a starter at Barcelona, ended with a goal in the World Cup final and the transformation of an ordinary player into a hero. But he was not even the first choice in his position for Spain, starting only one match in the campaign.

Federico Dimarco, the best player in the last Serie A, is one of the examples of players who had a great season and were left out of the list of 30 names.

It is contradictory, but the world champion goalkeeper was not the best of the past season, even though he conceded only one goal in the campaign and went seven matches without conceding.

Unai Simon was barely tested, with no memorable big save in the triumph, and only shone in his distribution and in anticipating danger.

Ironically, his understudy for Spain had a better season. David Raya produced a season of the highest level for English champions Arsenal, with decisive interventions, standout performances and a decisive role in the title and in reaching the European final.

#1821. Fans show concern for Mark Chapman after presenter's 'subdued' display on Match of the Day

Published: 11:22 GMT, 25 October 2026 | Updated: 11:22 GMT, 25 October 2026

Football fans have expressed their concern for Match of the Day host Mark Chapman after Saturday night's episode.

Chapman, 53, is a hugely popular presenter across TV and radio, working for the likes of BBC Radio 5 Live, Sky Sports and Match of the Day.

He is one of three co-hosts of the iconic show alongside Kelly Cates and Gabby Logan, and it was his turn in the presenting chair last night.

But, during the programme, which saw Steph Houghton and Martin Keown join Chapman as pundits, the 53-year-old appeared to be quite subdued and not as chatty or warm as usual.

This began with his muted and short introduction when Chapman looked down the camera and said: 'Five games on Match of the Day. Martin Keown and Steph Houghton are with us, and we start with Aston Villa against Manchester City, commentary from Simon Brotherton.'

Then, at the end of the programme, he signed off with his arms folded and added: 'That's it from us. Thanks to Martin and Steph. Goodnight.'

Reports broke this month that Chapman is dating his fellow MOTD co-host Kelly Cates (centre)

Chapman's subdued performance comes after reports broke last month of job cuts at BBC Sport, with 41 roles to be scrapped as part of a cost-cutting drive across the corporation.

Daily Mail Sport understands that figure includes the job of seven journalists - including four senior roles.

Sports bulletins on the 24-hour BBC News channel will also be lost as part of the drive to save money, staff were told last month.

The impending cuts - as reported by Daily Mail Sport - are likely to mean some staff have to reapply for their jobs, with some said to be considering strike action.

Meanwhile, news also broke earlier this month that Chapman was dating his fellow Match of the Day co-host Cates.

Although their shifts presenting the show do not overlap, Chapman and Cates did appear on Celebrity Gogglebox on the same sofa together in July, and since reports of their relationship emerged, they have been seen out in public.

Chapman was previously married to his wife Sara for 19 years until she died aged 44 in 2020 after a battle with cancer. They had three children together.

Cates, who is the daughter of Scotland and Liverpool legend Kenny Dalglish, was married to TV producer husband Tom Cates, with whom she has two children, but they split in 2021.
